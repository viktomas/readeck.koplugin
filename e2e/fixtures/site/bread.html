Baking Sourdough Bread at Home

Flour, water, salt and time.

Sourdough begins with a starter, a living culture of wild yeast and bacteria that you feed with flour and water every day until it doubles reliably.

The first week is the hardest, because the starter smells strange and rises unpredictably while the microbes compete for dominance in the jar.

Once the starter is active, mix it with more flour and water to build a levain, and leave it somewhere warm until it is bubbly and domed.

Part two

Combine the levain with the main dough and let it rest for an hour, a step bakers call the autolyse, before adding the salt.

Over the next four hours fold the dough every thirty minutes, stretching one side up and over the rest to build strength without kneading.

Shape the loaf gently, place it seam side up in a floured basket, and let it proof overnight in the refrigerator where the flavour deepens.

Bake in a preheated cast iron pot with the lid on for twenty minutes, then remove the lid so the crust can darken and crackle as it cools.
