The Lighthouse Keeper

A quiet life at the edge of the sea.

The lighthouse keeper climbed the spiral stairs every evening at dusk, counting each of the one hundred and twelve steps as his father had taught him.

Salt spray had etched patterns into the glass of the lantern room, and he polished them away with a soft cloth and a patience that the sea never matched.

Ships passed in the night without knowing his name, guided only by the steady sweep of light that he tended with the care of a gardener.

Part two

In winter the storms came from the north, hammering the tower with waves that rose higher than the gallery rail and fell back hissing into the dark.

He kept a logbook of every vessel he saw, noting the hour, the heading and the weather, though nobody had asked him to do so for many years.

When the supply boat arrived each fortnight it brought flour, lamp oil, newspapers weeks out of date, and occasionally a letter from his sister in the city.

The letters spoke of trams and theatres and a life that moved faster than the tides, and he read them twice before filing them in a tin box.

Some nights the fog was so thick that the beam seemed to stop an arm's length from the glass, and he sounded the horn until his ears rang.
