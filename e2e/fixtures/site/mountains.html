Walking the High Mountain Trail

Notes from a week above the tree line.

We left the valley before sunrise, when the meadows were still silver with frost and the only sound was the river running fast with snowmelt.

By mid morning the path had narrowed to a ledge cut into the rock, and we walked in single file with our hands brushing the cold stone.

The first hut appeared late in the afternoon, a low stone building with a tin roof and a stove that the warden lit as soon as we arrived.

Dinner was lentil soup and dark bread, eaten at long tables with climbers from four countries who shared maps and stories in a mix of languages.

Part two

On the third day clouds rolled in from the west and we crossed the pass in thick mist, following cairns that loomed suddenly out of the grey.

The descent was steep and loose, and we picked our way down scree slopes that slid beneath our boots with a sound like pouring gravel.

When we finally reached the village at the far end of the trail we sat by the fountain in the square and did not speak for a long time.
