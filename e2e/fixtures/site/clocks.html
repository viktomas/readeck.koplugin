How Mechanical Clocks Keep Time

Escapements, pendulums and gears.

A mechanical clock stores energy in a wound spring or a raised weight and releases it slowly through a train of gears that turn the hands.

The escapement is the heart of the mechanism, a small device that lets the gear train advance by one tooth at a time with each swing.

Without an escapement the spring would unwind in seconds, spinning the hands wildly and exhausting the stored energy all at once.

Part two

The pendulum sets the rhythm, because its period depends almost entirely on its length and hardly at all on how widely it swings.

Clockmakers adjust the rate by raising or lowering the bob with a small nut, lengthening the pendulum to slow the clock or shortening it to speed it.

Temperature changes the length of the rod, so fine clocks use compensating pendulums made of metals that expand in opposite directions.

A well made regulator can keep time to within a few seconds a month, a precision that guided navigators and railways for two centuries.
